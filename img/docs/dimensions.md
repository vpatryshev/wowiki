Dimensions for non-mathematicians

These days, or rather these years, the word "dimension" is used in very strange contexts, with strange meanings.
Like, e.g., if you read "Stranger in a Strange Land", you regularly encounter Valentine Michael Smith sending cars and other objects into "different dimensions".
People started to believe that there must be tricks to throw things to "parallel universes" (another strange thing) by using "other dimensions".
In the movies spaceships travel either "through a dimension" to another place, or to an "alternative universe" wich is in 'another dimention".

All this makes absolutely no sense.

Let me tell you something about dimensions.

In the world we live, we have width, length, and height. These are three dimensions of objects in our world.

Or we can only pay attention to  width and length, thus having two dimensions.
That's what we have on our screens. It takes two numbers to define a position of a point on the screen.

Adding one more dimension, and you have three dimensions: width, length, height. You need three numbers to define a position of an object. How you define these numbers,
depends on how you specify the coordinate system. Closest lower left corner of the room is (0,0,0); then you set the directions where the coordinates grow. (3,4,5) means probably
3 feet right, 4 feet forward, 5 feet up. Or whichever you choose. The number of dimensions does not depend on how you define coordinates. You can add more, like, distance from that corner,
(0,0,0), but it's not an independent coordinate. It can be calculated from its other coordinates. This space we are talking about is three-dimensional.

Is everything in real life three-dimensional? Definitely not. At times you need to write down more numbers to specify the situation. (x,y,z,t) - coordinates and the moment of time,
probably the moment when our object was there.

Remember Xeno's Arrow Paradox? There's an arrow at a certain position. How is it different from another arrow that is moving, and is at the same position at a certain moment of time?
If we only know their positions, we can't tell them apart. what is the difference then?
The difference is in their velocities. One has a zero velocity, the other one has a non-zero velocity.
Velocity is specified by its three components: vertical speed, left-right speed, and forward speed.

So we need not three, but six numbers to describe the state of the arrow. Good, we have six dimensions!
